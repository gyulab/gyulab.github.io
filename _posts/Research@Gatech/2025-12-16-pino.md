# Motivation: TCAD Bottleneck for Fe-VNAND Retention Analysis

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Ferroelectric FET (FeFET)-based vertical NAND (**Fe-VNAND**) is a promising candidate to overcome the Z-scaling limitations of 3D NAND with lower programming voltages. However, its **data retention** is hindered by a complex, time-dependent interplay between **ferroelectric depolarization** and **charge detrapping** at the dielectric interfaces. Optimizing the gate stack against these effects requires exploring a huge design space—geometry, temperature, and retention time.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Conventional Technology Computer-Aided Design (TCAD) is accurate but computationally prohibitive: a single rigorous simulation sweeping temperature and time can take **over 24 hours**, and the cost scales linearly with every additional variable. To solve this, we developed a **Physics-Informed Neural Operator (PINO)-based AI surrogate model on the NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo framework** that predicts 2D physics maps, I<sub>D</sub>-V<sub>G</sub> curves, and V<sub>th</sub> retention loss with near-TCAD accuracy in seconds.

<br><br>

# Device and TCAD Training Data

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;We study a single FeFET with an interlayer-engineered gate stack, where a **Tunnel Dielectric Layer (TDL)** is inserted between ferroelectric layers (**HZO/TDL/HZO**). During erase, charges accumulate at the HZO/TDL interface via Fowler-Nordheim tunneling and counteract the depolarization field. During retention, these screening charges detrap, which exacerbates the depolarization field and accelerates polarization loss.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Training data were generated with Synopsys Sentaurus TCAD (ferroelectric Preisach model + FN tunneling, retention up to 10<sup>4</sup> s) by sweeping three inputs, giving **only 120 TCAD sweeps** in total:

| Input Sweep Parameter | Values |
| :--- | :--- |
| HZO Thickness | {5, 6, 7, 8, 9} nm |
| Temperature | {300, 358, 400, 473} K |
| Retention Time | {0, 1e0, 1e1, 1e2, 1e3, 1e4} s |

<br>

For each condition, we extract the I<sub>D</sub>-V<sub>G</sub> curve and internal 2D physics maps: **electrostatic potential, polarization, and trapped electron/hole densities** from the gate stack (HZO/TDL/HZO), and the **current density** from the entire device to capture channel conductance changes.

<br><br>

# Methodology: Physics-Informed Neural Operator (PINO)

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Our model is not a "black box." Instead of directly mapping scalar inputs to I<sub>D</sub>-V<sub>G</sub> curves, it first predicts **intermediate physics maps**, which makes the surrogate a physically interpretable digital twin and acts as a physical regularizer for the downstream readout.

#### Architecture
![PINO Architecture](/assets/img/research/pino_architecture.png "image"){:.profile}<br>

The pipeline consists of three phases:
* **Phase 1 – Input Embedding:** Scalar device inputs (t<sub>HZO</sub>, T, log(τ)) are embedded into an input tensor.
* **Phase 2 – PINO Physics Surrogate:** A Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) backbone performs global convolutions in the Fourier domain and maps the inputs to resolution-invariant 2D physics fields (J, Q<sub>e,h</sub>, V, P) on a 256×256 grid. To avoid overfitting the sparse dataset, the FNO is strictly regularized: **4 Fourier layers, modes = 12, latent width = 32**.
* **Phase 3 – IV-Net Readout:** A shallow CNN map encoder extracts features from the physics maps, while the original scalar inputs bypass the surrogate through a parallel MLP (**scalar context injection**). The two are fused to predict the final I<sub>D</sub>-V<sub>G</sub> curve. This residual path preserves global thermodynamic context (e.g., bake temperature) that cannot be extracted from purely electrostatic 2D maps.

#### Physics-Informed Loss Function
The key difference between a purely data-driven FNO and a PINO is the dual-loss objective:

<center>$$
\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{data} + \lambda \cdot \mathcal{L}_{physics}
$$</center>
<br>

where $$\mathcal{L}_{data}$$ is the L2 error against TCAD ground truth, and $$\mathcal{L}_{physics}$$ consists of two components:

1. **Poisson Residual:** enforces that the predicted potential $$\phi$$, charge density $$\rho$$, and ferroelectric polarization $$P$$ obey electrostatics.
<center>$$
\mathcal{L}_{Poisson} = \nabla \cdot (\varepsilon \nabla \phi) + (\rho - \nabla \cdot P)
$$</center>
2. **Monotonicity Constraint:** enforces the monotonic decay of remnant polarization and trapped charge over retention time, penalizing unphysical glitches or oscillations.
<center>$$
\mathcal{L}_{mono} = ReLU\left(\frac{\partial |P_r|}{\partial \tau}\right) + ReLU\left(\frac{\partial |Q_{it}|}{\partial \tau}\right)
$$</center>
<br>

This physics-informed loss constrains the hypothesis space when training data are sparse, which is exactly the regime of expensive semiconductor simulations.

# Results

#### 1. Reconstruction Accuracy of 2D Physics Maps
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Validated against TCAD, the trained PINO reconstructs 2D profiles of electrostatic potential, trapped charge, and current density with a relative error of **~0.1%**.

#### 2. I<sub>D</sub>-V<sub>G</sub> Prediction and Retention Analysis
* **V<sub>th</sub> Accuracy:** R<sup>2</sup> > **0.998** for V<sub>th</sub> (at I<sub>D</sub> = 100 nA) over all 120 sweep conditions.
* **Unseen Temperature:** The model produces physically consistent ΔV<sub>th</sub> retention curves at an **unseen 350 K**, lying between the 300 K and 400 K TCAD baselines.
* **Physics vs. Data-Driven:** The purely data-driven FNO overfits the sparse dataset and shows unstable ripples across the transfer curve, while the PINO predicts a smooth I<sub>D</sub>-V<sub>G</sub> response, improving RMSE by **> 40%** (0.080 → 0.047).

#### 3. Computational Efficiency

| Metric | TCAD | AI Surrogate |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Single sweep | 30 min | ~0.1 s (real-time) |
| Full sweep | 60 hrs | ~10 s |
| DSE coverage | Discrete (prone to blind spots) | Continuous (mesh-free interpolation) |
| Cost of optimization | Prohibitive | Minimal |

<br>
(TCAD: Intel Xeon Platinum 8470N 52-core CPU, AI surrogate: NVIDIA Titan RTX 24 GB GPU)

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A full retention sweep goes from **60 hours to ~10 seconds**, a speedup of four orders of magnitude (**> 10,000×**).

# Open-Source Demo: PINO for Semiconductor

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;To make the workflow reproducible, we released a demo repository, <a href="https://github.com/gyulab/PINO-for-Semiconductor" target="_blank">PINO-for-Semiconductor</a>, that applies the same NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo-based FNO/PINO pipeline to **3D SONOS NAND flash** devices. Watch the tutorial video at the top of this post for a walkthrough.

```
PINO-for-Semiconductor/
├── code/
│   ├── train_fno_model_sonos.py    # Standard FNO, MSE loss (data-driven only)
│   ├── train_pino_model_sonos.py   # PINO with Poisson residual loss
│   ├── inference_time_sweep.py     # Time-evolution animation of defect charge density
│   └── inference_pino_time.py      # PINO time-sweep inference
├── docs/
│   └── AI-Surrogate-modeling-tutorial.pdf
└── results/                        # FNO vs. PINO visualizations (below)
```

In `train_pino_model_sonos.py`, the Poisson equation $$\varepsilon \nabla^2 \phi + \rho = 0$$ is implemented as a custom PhysicsNeMo `Node`, and its residual is driven to zero alongside the data loss. The training data (AMAT Ginestra simulations) are not included due to EULA/NDA restrictions.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Before the ICMC paper, we explored the framework on other configurations.

# Summary and Future Work

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;We developed a physics-informed AI surrogate model that faithfully accelerates Fe-VNAND retention analysis:
* **Efficiency:** Trained on only 120 TCAD sweeps, with strict regularization to generalize to unseen temporal, thermal, and geometric regimes.
* **Speed:** > 10,000× speedup (60 hrs → ~10 s for a full retention sweep), enabling continuous design space exploration.
* **Physics:** Governed by Poisson's equation and monotonicity constraints, eliminating unphysical artifacts and improving RMSE by > 40% over a data-driven FNO.
* **Compact Modeling:** Serves as a physics-consistent data engine for compact model parameter extraction and LUT generation for reliability-aware SPICE simulation.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Next, we are extending this work to full-scale 3D Fe-VNAND string arrays in a journal version. Stay tuned!
